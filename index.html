The Cumulative LDL Burden Concept
The main determinant of ASCVD risk is not the LDL-C value itself but the integral (area under the curve) of duration of exposure × LDL-C level.
Long-term exposure to varying LDL-C levels has a greater impact on ASCVD outcomes than observed in RCTs, driven by duration of exposure.
It is therefore important to optimize healthy lifestyle habits from a young age and prevent the onset of dyslipidemia as early as possible.
2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline — Section 4.1 (Lifestyle), Synopsis
Mendelian Randomization Evidence
People genetically predisposed to lifelong low LDL-C have lower lifetime ASCVD risk. Conversely, high cholesterol in childhood persists into adulthood and increases midlife ASCVD risk.
Early detection and treatment safely and effectively lower LDL-C, confirmed in both short/medium-term RCTs and long-term follow-up studies.
Section 3.2 Supportive Text #2; 4.2.8.1 Children and Adolescents
Key Cohort & Trial Evidence
CARDIA Cohort Coronary Artery Risk Development in Young Adults
Even a modest increase in LDL-C area under the curve was associated with a significant increase in ASCVD events over 16 years of follow-up. Confirms the potential for early intervention to improve long-term event rates.
FHS-OS Cohort Framingham Heart Study Offspring
Cumulative exposure to dyslipidemia in young adulthood dose-dependently increases subsequent coronary heart disease (CHD) risk.
FOURIER-OLE Open-Label Extension · evolocumab
Patients started on evolocumab from the outset had a lower MACE incidence than those switched over after placebo → confirms a legacy effect of early treatment initiation. The timing of treatment initiation affects outcomes.
CTT Meta-analysis
Each 1 mmol/L (38.7 mg/dL) reduction in LDL-C is associated with roughly a 22% relative risk reduction in ASCVD events. Five-year major coronary event rates fall linearly as the absolute achieved LDL-C level decreases — even at a baseline LDL of 77 mg/dL or below, further lowering yields a 21% risk reduction.
Managing Cumulative LDL Burden by Life Stage (2026 ACC/AHA)
1 Childhood & Adolescence Section 4.2.8.1 · COR 1 B-NR
Lipid abnormalities occur in 1 in 5 adolescents; the FH phenotype in 1 in 250–300. Childhood cholesterol tracks into adulthood. Universal lipid screening recommended at age 9–11. Carotid intima-media thickness (cIMT) is detectable from age 8–10. Early detection → early lifestyle intervention is the foundation for reducing long-term risk.
2 Young Adulthood (age 18–39) Section 4.2.8.2 · COR 1 B-NR
Optimizing diet, physical activity, and weight is recommended to reduce cumulative atherogenic lipid exposure and lifetime ASCVD risk (Class I). Moderate-intensity statin is reasonable if 30-year risk ≥10% or LDL-C 160–189 mg/dL (Class 2a). Young ASCVD patients who don't meet VHR criteria still carry high recurrence risk due to long lifetime cumulative LDL exposure ahead.
3 Middle-Aged Adulthood (40–74) Section 4.2.3
Combine PREVENT 10-year/30-year risk assessment with risk-enhancing factors and CAC. Even if 10-year risk <3%, LLT may be considered to reduce cumulative exposure if atherogenic lipid burden is high. The 30-year risk estimate helps visualize long-term cumulative risk in younger middle-aged adults.
4 Older Adulthood (age ≥75) Section 4.2.8.3 · COR 1 C-EO
Age alone should not be the basis for discontinuing LLT. Continuing LLT is reasonable in patients ≥75 with life expectancy ≥2.5 years. An individualized approach accounting for already-accumulated atherosclerotic burden. Incorporate frailty, polypharmacy, functional status, and patient preference into shared decision-making.

Clinical Significance of 30-Year Risk (PREVENT 30-year risk)
Even in younger adults (age 30–59) with low 10-year risk, moderate-intensity statin is reasonable if 30-year risk ≥10% (Class 2a, C-LD). A recommendation that directly applies the cumulative LDL burden concept to clinical decisions — "even if 10-year risk is low right now, reducing lifetime exposure still provides benefit."
ASCVD risk = LDL-C level × duration of exposure

CPR Framework — 2026 ACC/AHA Guideline (Section 4.2.3)
After estimating 10-year ASCVD risk, when clinical uncertainty remains or the patient is hesitant about a treatment decision, CAC measurement is useful for risk reclassification in borderline-to-intermediate-risk patients. This approach is termed the "CPR Framework" (Calculate–Personalize–Reclassify).

Beyond absolute 10-year/30-year risk, comorbidities, life expectancy, patient preference, and potential net benefit are incorporated into shared decision-making.

ApoB — Refining Care After the LDL-C Target Is Met (Section 3.3)
ApoB measurement is useful during LLT for identifying residual lipoprotein-related risk, particularly in adults with ASCVD, CKM syndrome, TG ≥150, diabetes, or on-treatment LDL-C <70 mg/dL (COR 2a).
Discordance between LDL-C and ApoB is common with elevated TG or metabolic syndrome. Using the Martin/Hopkins method to calculate LDL-C substantially reduces discordance compared with Friedewald, but ApoB still provides additional information.
ApoB can be measured in the non-fasting state and is standardized, making it practical.

ApoB — The Pathology of Particle Number
1
Every atherogenic lipoprotein (LDL, VLDL, IDL, Lp(a)) contains exactly one ApoB-100 → ApoB = total number of atherogenic particles
2
Even with the same LDL-C, a higher particle count (ApoB) means higher risk — the core of the LDL discordance phenomenon
3
It is the particle itself that infiltrates and accumulates in the arterial wall → particle number is the causal driver of atherosclerosis
💡 At the same LDL-C of 100 mg/dL, many small particles (ApoB↑) are dangerous, while fewer large particles (normal ApoB) are relatively safe.
Lp(a) — A Triple Pathologic Mechanism
A
Atherogenic mechanism
Contains ApoB → infiltrates the intima, carries oxidized phospholipids (OxPL) → activates macrophages
B
Thrombogenic mechanism
apo(a) kringle structure resembles plasminogen → competitively inhibits tPA → reduces fibrinolysis
C
Promotes calcific aortic stenosis
OxPL → accelerates aortic valve calcification (progresses independently of LDL lowering)
⚠️ Lp(a) is not lowered by statins; PCSK9i reduces it 20–30%, and newer siRNAs (olpasiran, muvalaplin) reduce it >90%.

▸ What Is LDL/non-HDL Discordance?
Definition: LDL-C and non-HDL-C fall into different risk categories.
In other words, one marker is within target while the other exceeds it.

Cause: Because non-HDL-C = LDL-C + VLDL-C + IDL-C + Lp(a),
when VLDL remnants increase — with ↑TG, diabetes, insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, or chronic kidney disease —
non-HDL-C can exceed target even when LDL-C is normal.

Target comparison:
 · Very high risk: LDL <55 mg/dL / non-HDL <85 mg/dL
 · High risk: LDL <70 mg/dL / non-HDL <100 mg/dL
 · Moderate risk: LDL <100 mg/dL / non-HDL <130 mg/dL

Clinical significance: If non-HDL-C exceeds target despite the LDL-C target being met, it indicates residual cardiovascular risk from remnant cholesterol (VLDL remnants, etc.).
In this situation, direct ApoB measurement further confirms the true atherogenic particle burden.

🫀 What Is CKM Syndrome?
A unifying syndrome linking cardiovascular, kidney, and metabolic disease through shared pathophysiology (obesity, insulin resistance, chronic inflammation).
Formally defined as Stage 0–4 in the 2023 AHA Presidential Advisory (Ndumele CE et al.).

▸ Rationale — the Statin Paradox in Dialysis
4D study and AURORA trial: significantly lowered LDL-C but did not demonstrate reduced major cardiovascular events
SHARP: 17% event reduction confirmed in non-dialysis CKD, but the dialysis subgroup was not significant (HR 0.90)
→ In dialysis patients, sudden death and heart failure — not atherosclerosis — dominate cardiovascular mortality

Statin Treatment Effect — NNT Comparison Primary · Secondary Prevention ▶
NNT (Number Needed to Treat) is the number of patients who must be treated to prevent one event. A lower NNT means greater treatment effect, and NNT falls as baseline risk rises. For the same RRR (relative risk reduction), a larger absolute risk (ARR) yields a greater primary-prevention benefit.
Bar length is proportional to ARR (absolute risk reduction) — longer means greater treatment effect. Not an absolute prevention rate.

Pharmacologic SAMS — Mechanism and Clinical Application
► Mechanism
① CoQ10 depletion
Mevalonate pathway blockade → ↓ubiquinone synthesis → ↓mitochondrial ATP production, ↑oxidative stress
② Direct myocyte toxicity
↑Muscle penetration of lipophilic statins (simvastatin, lovastatin) → destabilizes myocyte membrane, induces apoptosis
③ ↓Selenoprotein synthesis
Reduced isopentenyl-PP → deficiency of antioxidant selenoproteins (e.g., GPx) → oxidative muscle damage
④ Chloride channel dysfunction
Increased sarcolemmal excitability → muscle stiffness and cramping

⚠ The prescribing recommendation and the NNT figure may differ
This NNT is calculated using only the PREVENT 2023 10-year risk score × CTT RRR, so it may diverge from the prescribing recommendation above.
· LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL — an absolute indication outside PREVENT's applicable range (LDL 70–189); high-intensity statin is recommended even at low PREVENT risk, which can make the resulting NNT appear high
· Diabetes / CKD stage 3–4 — already factored into the PREVENT equation, so NNT is calculated accurately. However, the guideline's LLT recommendation applies to ages 40–75 and follows a risk-independent treatment pathway, so the clinical meaning of the recommendation strength and the NNT figure may appear to diverge
· With risk enhancers (RE) — REs are not part of the PREVENT inputs, so any RE-driven increase in recommended intensity is not reflected in the NNT calculation (see the adjusted NNT*)
· Secondary prevention — baseline risk is estimated as a fixed value (~25–40%), so individual variation exists
· Adjusted NNT* — applies only the single strongest-evidence factor among CAC, Lp(a), and RE (in that priority order: CAC > Lp(a) > RE). Overlapping multiple factors risks over-adjustment due to violated independence assumptions
The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline specifies that NNT-based discussion is most valid in the borderline-to-intermediate-risk (3–10%) primary-prevention range.
